---
title: "Refunds, Returns, and Credit Notes"
description: "Three related but different things — giving money back, taking an item back, and recording that you've paid a customer back — and how they fit together"
---

When something goes wrong with an order, three different (but related) things might need to
happen, and it's easy to mix them up:

- A **Refund** is about the *money* — agreeing to give some or all of it back.
- A **Return** is about the *item* — the customer physically sending a product back to you.
- A **Credit Note** is the record that you've actually *paid the customer back*, once a refund has
  been agreed.

You don't always need all three — sometimes you'll only issue a refund with no item coming back
(for example, the item never arrived), and sometimes a customer sends an item back without asking
for money (for example, an exchange). All three live inside the order they belong to, and each
also has its own screen that lists every order going through that process, across your whole shop.

## Refunds

A refund is how you agree to give the customer some or all of their money back for an order.

### Issuing a refund

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Open the order and click **Refunds** in the Actions card (in the Financial group).
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Since there's no refund yet, you'll see the **Issue refund** form. Enter an **Amount** (up to
    the order's subtotal) and, optionally, a **Reason** (e.g. "Item damaged"). You're reminded that
    the customer will be notified at their email address.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Click **Issue refund**. The order now shows a refund with status **Pending**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Moving a refund forward

Once a refund exists on an order, opening the **Refunds** action again shows its current amount
and status, with buttons that change depending on where it is:

- **Pending** — click **Approve** to move it forward, or **Reject** to decline it. Approving a
  refund is described in the app as "awaiting item return" — the usual next step is to also handle
  the physical item coming back, using the Returns process below.
- **Approved** — click **Accept** to finalize it. Accepting a refund automatically creates a
  **Credit Note** for that amount (see below) — this is the step that actually commits to paying
  the customer back.
- **Rejected** — the refund is closed with no further action.

Every one of these steps notifies the customer automatically and is recorded on the order's
activity timeline.

### The Refunds screen

Beyond handling a refund from inside its order, there's also a dedicated screen listing every
order in your shop that has a refund on it. From there you can **Search refunds…** by order
number, customer name, or reason, filter by **Status** (with a count next to each one), sort
**Newest first**/**Oldest first**, and approve, reject, or accept a refund directly from the list
without opening the order — the same **Approve**, **Reject**, and **Accept** buttons as on the
order itself.

## Returns

A return is about the physical item, not the money — it tracks a customer sending a product back
to you.

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Once an order is **Delivered** or **Fulfilled**, a **Request Return** button appears in the
    order's Fulfillment actions (it only shows up if the order doesn't already have a return on
    it).
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Click it and enter a **Reason** (e.g. "Item damaged in transit"), then click **Create return**.
    The return is created with status **Requested**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A return then moves through its own set of stages as the physical item makes its way back to you:
**Requested → Approved → Received → Accepted**, or **Rejected** instead of Approved if you decline
the request. A card on the order screen shows the return's current status, its reason, and any
notes, along with how long ago it last changed.

There's also a dedicated **Returns** screen listing every order with a return on it, where you can
move each one forward with the matching button for its current stage — **Approve** or **Reject**
a newly **Requested** return, **Mark Received** once the item physically arrives back from an
**Approved** return, and **Accept Return** once you've checked it over and it's truly
**Received**.

## Credit Notes

A credit note is the record that a refund has actually been paid back to the customer — it's
created for you automatically the moment you **Accept** a refund (see above), so you never create
one by hand.

A credit note has its own reference code and starts out with status **Pending** — agreed, but not
yet paid. Once you've actually sent the money back to the customer (by whatever means you use
outside the app — bank transfer, cash, store credit, and so on), open the **Credit Note**
action on the order (it shows the amount, its reference, and its status) and click **Pay back**.
This marks it **Paid**, and the order's activity timeline records that the credit was paid back.

There's also a dedicated **Credit Notes** screen listing every order with a credit note on it,
where you can see its reference, amount, status, and date, and click **Pay back** on any that are
still **Pending** without opening the order.

## How the three fit together

A typical damaged-item scenario runs through all three, in this order:

1. The order is delivered. The customer wants to send the item back and be refunded.
2. You click **Request Return** on the order and record why. You then **Approve** it, **Mark
   Received** once it physically arrives, and **Accept Return** once you've checked it.
3. Separately, you **Issue** a **Refund** for the agreed amount, **Approve** it, then **Accept**
   it — accepting creates a **Credit Note**.
4. Once you've actually sent the money, you mark the **Credit Note** as **Paid back**.

Refunds, returns, and credit notes are tracked separately on the order (an order can have a return
without a refund, or a refund without a return), but together they give you — and the customer —
a complete record of exactly what was agreed and what's actually been done about it.
